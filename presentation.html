Why Your Gut Biome Needs Your Help!

Trillions of microscopic organisms live inside your digestive system. They regulate immunity, digestion, and mood. Here is what happens when they get disrupted.

💥

What Causes Biome Disruption?

Events like colonoscopy prep, broad-spectrum antibiotics, or food poisoning flush or alter friendly bacterial populations overnight.

🔬 Medical Reference: NIH / PMC Reference — Post-antibiotic microbiota dysbiosis
🏛️

Hippocrates Was Onto Something

“All disease begins in the gut.”

Modern microbiomics confirms that intestinal wall integrity directly influences systemic inflammatory health.

🥗

Prebiotics & Soluble Fiber

Soluble fibers from foods like oats, cooked apples, and bananas pass into the colon to feed beneficial Bifidobacteria strains.

🔬 Medical Reference: Mayo Clinic Microbiomics Research — Prebiotic fiber roles
🥣

Probiotics: Seeding Live Microbes

Fermented foods like yogurt, kefir, miso, and sauerkraut introduce live transient strains that lower gut pH and suppress harmful bacteria.

🛑

What to Avoid Early On

High simple sugars, artificial sweeteners, and heavy saturated fats can fuel yeast overgrowth (like Candida) or cause cramping on an empty stomach.

🍵

Soothing the Mucosal Barrier

Warm bone broths, mineral veggie soups, and herbal teas (like fennel or ginger) help soothe raw tissue while villi recover.

🧠

The Gut-Brain Axis

Over 90% of the body's serotonin is produced in the gut! Protecting your microbiome helps support mood, energy, and mental focus.

🔬 Medical Reference: Johns Hopkins Medicine — Brain-Gut Connection
🍌

Resistant Starch Magic

Slightly green bananas and cooled cooked rice contain RS2 resistant starch—a primary fuel source for gut microbes to make protective short-chain fatty acids.

📅

Recovery Takes Time

Colonoscopy recovery takes 3 days, food bug recovery takes ~7 days, and post-antibiotic rebuilding takes 2–4 weeks. Phased eating makes the transition seamless.
